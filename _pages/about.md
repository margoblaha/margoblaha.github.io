## In brief

*Epistemic Margins* is a place for questions that lead me somewhere unexpected.

I write about sociology, philosophy, psychology, media, culture, globalisation, relationships, and the details of everyday life that if you look closely reveal something larger about the world we all inhabit. Where does the personal meet the social? How we learn what to want, how we understand one another, how cultural ideas travel? How technology shapes what we notice, and how the world we live can shape the people we become.

The things we experience as individual and ordinary often have histories, structures and assumptions worth examining, and this is a space to follow interdisciplinary connections wherever they lead.

## Why do I do this

My curiosity was what made me want to become a researcher, where we learn to ask better questions, look for evidence, and understand the systems and relationships that shape the world around us.
Here, I follow that curiosity wherever it leads. Sometimes it means looking at a personal experience through a sociological lens, questioning the assumptions behind something seen as ordinary, or connecting a philosophical idea to something happening in contemporary culture. I would like to explore the links between things and in what becomes visible when we look at the familiar from a different angle.

## How I write

I think most ideas begin with ordinary life. For me, many take shape somewhere on a commute, looking _dramatically_ out of a bus or train window and letting my mind wander. I love having that time to think about absolutely nothing in particular, to follow a thought without needing to turn it into anything useful. This kind of unstructured thinking feels increasingly valuable in a world where we are constantly flooded with information and expected to make every spare moment productive. Not every thought needs a purpose, and not every interest needs to become a skill or a side hustle. Of course, there is a slight contradiction in starting a to publicly write about all this! Apparently, even my attempts to resist productivity culture have resulted in a project. I suppose I could have just looked out of the window, but here we are.

I love to write essays and reflections where I try to distinguish what the evidence currently supports, and why, from what a theory can help us see and what remains, for now, my own interpretation.

## Why *Epistemic Margins*?

Our knowledge has boundaries, but how do we know where those boundaries lie? And who decides where they should be drawn?

_Epistemic Margins_ is a space for exploring these questions.

_Epistemic_ refers to knowledge, thereofre to how we acquire it, justify it, question it, and decide what counts as _knowing_. _Margins_ evokes the limits of our own understanding and the notes we write alongside established ideas, which are small spaces for questioning.

Yet when I was wiritng this I thought, is there a paradox in the very act of defining something? To define is also to delimit. Definitions help us make sense of the world, but they can also constrain how we see it. However, I think that the danger is not necessarily in drawing boundaries, but in mistaking the boundaries of our concepts for the boundaries of reality itself.

This is a space for _thoughts in progress_ and an invitation to explore, to remain curious and to take ideas seriously while leaving room for uncertainty.
